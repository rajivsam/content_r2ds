If you are interested in how data science and AI actually get built in the real world — where business constraints, data quality, and operating models matter as much as modeling technique — this is the place to start.

{: style="text-align: justify"}
I write about the gap between the polished story of AI and the practical work of producing reliable, explainable, and operationally useful systems.

### Recent Essays

- [Beyond the Agent Illusion: Leveraging AI Systems for Real Operational Value](https://r2dssanity.substack.com/p/beyond-the-agent-illusion-leveraging?r=3ncgel)  
  A comparison of two ways to build intelligent systems: the AI-first, agentic view and the data-science view. The essay argues that while there are niches such as self-driving cars and robotics where the agentic approach is essential, most enterprise operations are better served by the data-science view when decisions must be explained, trusted, and understood by business operators.

- [From Business Use Case to Technical Implementation](https://r2dssanity.substack.com/p/from-business-use-case-to-technical?r=3ncgel)  
  Turning business problems into tractable analytical systems, with attention to constraints, data realities, and measurable outcomes.

- [From Task-Specific Data to Modeling](https://r2dssanity.substack.com/p/from-task-specific-data-to-modeling?r=3ncgel)  
  How raw operational data becomes the modeling substrate, and why task framing matters as much as the algorithm.

- [Modeling and Featurization](https://r2dssanity.substack.com/p/modeling-and-featurization?r=3ncgel)  
  The design choices that shape model quality, especially where feature engineering determines whether the system captures signal or noise.

{: style="text-align: justify"}
These essays are intended to be practical, opinionated, and grounded in applied analytics rather than hype. They sit alongside the consulting and methodology work on this site, and they are meant to help teams think more clearly about where data science adds value and where it does not.
